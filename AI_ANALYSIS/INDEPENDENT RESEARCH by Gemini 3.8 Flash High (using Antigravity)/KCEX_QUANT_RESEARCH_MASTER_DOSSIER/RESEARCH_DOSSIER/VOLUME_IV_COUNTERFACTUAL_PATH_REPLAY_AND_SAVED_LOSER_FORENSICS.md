## 1. Methodology of the Counterfactual Path Matrix

A fundamental flaw in traditional backtesting is path-dependency: when a trade with a wider stop stays open longer, it skips subsequent signals that a tighter-stop system would have taken. To eliminate this distortion, we implemented a **counterfactual path replay engine**:

1. **Signal Isolation**: Every valid signal generated by `STOCH_RSI FAST_SCALP` on TRUMP (July 1–24, 2026) was identified at its exact timestamp. Total sample: **4,436 discrete signals**.
2. **Forward Tick Stream Replay**: For each signal, the simulator opened a forward millisecond tick trade stream starting from entry timestamp $T_0$ up to $T_0 + 3600\text{ seconds}$.
3. **Simultaneous Barrier Evaluation**: The forward price trajectory was simultaneously monitored against seven discrete stop loss boundaries:
   $$\text{SL} \in [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 10]\text{ ticks}, \quad \text{TP} = +2\text{ ticks}$$
4. **Metric Capture**: For every signal, we recorded:
   - Maximum Favorable Excursion (MFE) in ticks
   - Maximum Adverse Excursion (MAE) in ticks
   - Exact outcome (WIN or LOSS) under each stop loss
   - Time to Take Profit and Time to Stop Loss

## 3. The Exact Net Economics: SL = 5 vs SL = 2

Why did SL = 5 generate more profit than SL = 2 on the July discovery sample?
We can calculate the exact arithmetical contribution of each trade:

### 3.1 Payoff Mechanics
- A Winning Trade pays $+2\text{ ticks} = +0.0004\text{ USDT}$.
- A Losing Trade at $\text{SL} = 2$ costs $-2\text{ ticks} = -0.0004\text{ USDT}$.
- A Losing Trade at $\text{SL} = 5$ costs $-5\text{ ticks} = -0.0010\text{ USDT}$.

### 3.2 The Saved Trades Benefit
For the 1,155 trades in `SAVED_BY_SL5`:
- Under $\text{SL} = 2$, each was a $-2\text{ tick}$ loss.
- Under $\text{SL} = 5$, each became a $+2\text{ tick}$ win.
- Net gain per saved trade: $+2 - (-2) = \mathbf{+4\text{ ticks}}$ ($+0.0008\text{ USDT}$).
$$\text{Total Value Created by Saved Trades} = 1,155 \times +4\text{ ticks} = \mathbf{+4,620\text{ ticks}} = \mathbf{+0.9240\text{ USDT}}$$

### 3.3 The Damaged Trades Penalty
For the 1,022 trades in `EXTRA_DAMAGE_SL5`:
- Under $\text{SL} = 2$, each was stopped at $-2\text{ ticks}$.
- Under $\text{SL} = 5$, each was stopped at $-5\text{ ticks}$.
- Net penalty per damaged trade: $-5 - (-2) = \mathbf{-3\text{ ticks}}$ ($-0.0006\text{ USDT}$).
$$\text{Total Value Destroyed by Damaged Trades} = 1,022 \times -3\text{ ticks} = \mathbf{-3,066\text{ ticks}} = \mathbf{-0.6132\text{ USDT}}$$

### 3.4 The Net Balance
$$\text{Net Difference (SL=5 vs SL=2)} = +4,620\text{ ticks} - 3,066\text{ ticks} = \mathbf{+1,554\text{ ticks}} = \mathbf{+0.3108\text{ USDT}}$$

## 4. The Fragility Proof: The 1.13:1 Ratio

Notice the critical ratio between Saved Trades and Damaged Trades in July:
$$\text{Ratio} = \frac{\text{Saved Trades}}{\text{Damaged Trades}} = \frac{1,155}{1,022} = \mathbf{1.13}$$

In July, for every 10 trades that blew through the 5-tick stop, **only 11.3 trades were saved** between 2 and 5 ticks!

### The Break-Even Threshold:
To break even between $\text{SL} = 5$ and $\text{SL} = 2$:
$$\text{Saved} \times 4 = \text{Damaged} \times 3 \implies \frac{\text{Saved}}{\text{Damaged}} = \frac{3}{4} = \mathbf{0.75}$$
However, this assumes an identical number of total signals. In practice, when candle volatility expands:
- In higher volatility (e.g. January with 5.1-tick bar range), the probability of adverse excursion exceeding 5 ticks rises sharply.
- If just **150 trades** shift from `SAVED_BY_SL5` into `EXTRA_DAMAGE_SL5` (from $1,155 \to 1,005$ saved and $1,022 \to 1,172$ damaged):
  $$\text{Net} = (1,005 \times 4) - (1,172 \times 3) = 4,020 - 3,516 = +504\text{ ticks}$$
- If 250 trades shift:
  $$\text{Net} = (905 \times 4) - (1,272 \times 3) = 3,620 - 3,816 = \mathbf{-196\text{ ticks (NET LOSS!)}}$$

This mathematically explains why SL = 5 collapsed in January, February, and March 2026. As market volatility widened the distribution of Maximum Adverse Excursions, the damaged trades overwhelmed the saved trades, rendering SL = 5 severely inferior to SL = 2.
