# 1. What your backtests actually discovered

Your experiments have effectively isolated three separate problems.

### Problem A: The entries are often not the main problem

Both strategies can generate a surprisingly high raw win rate when positions are allowed to remain open indefinitely. EMA_CROSSOVER reached 85.34% WR, while STOCH_RSI reached 84.75%. But the payoff asymmetry means those numbers are dangerously close to the mathematical survival boundary.  

For the nominal $0.0004 win versus $0.0020 loss configuration:

$$
E = W(0.0004)-(1-W)(0.0020)
$$

so:

$$
W_{BE}=83.33\%
$$

That means a strategy with 85% WR isn't swimming in edge. It is walking across a very thin bridge.

And the bridge gets even stranger because your -25% ROE stop is **not a fixed number of ticks**. As TRUMP's price changed, the same ROE stop represented very different absolute price movements. Your dossiers show approximately 16.7 ticks at $5, 10.7 ticks around $3.2, 7.3 ticks around $2.2 and 4.8 ticks around $1.45. 

So your actual strategy geometry is changing with the asset price.

---

### Problem B: The biggest enemy is time

This is the most convincing finding in the entire dataset.

For EMA_CROSSOVER, winning trades had median duration around 8.4 seconds, while losing trades had median duration around 374 seconds in the no-timeout run. The analysis found that once a scalp survives beyond roughly 38-45 seconds, the conditional probability of eventual TP collapses.  

STOCH_RSI shows essentially the same pathology. In one cross-run analysis, the reported conditional expectancy becomes approximately zero around 38-40 seconds and negative thereafter. 

So the important variable is not merely:

`"Did the signal work?"`

It is:

`"Did the signal produce the required displacement quickly?"`

That is a fundamentally different trading system.

---

### Problem C: The fixed 2-tick target becomes physically inappropriate

This appears repeatedly across the dossiers.

When ATR compressed below roughly 0.0025-0.0028, a 2-tick target became larger than, or comparable to, the expected movement of an entire 1-minute candle. During the compression phase, performance collapsed spectacularly.  

The important point is:

**ATR is not merely a risk filter. In your system it is a target-feasibility variable.**

That distinction matters enormously.

# 3. Strategy #1: EMA_CROSSOVER

## New identity

I would turn EMA_CROSSOVER into a **pure momentum continuation scalp**.

I would *not* allow it to compete with STOCH_RSI for range-trading signals.

The reports repeatedly show EMA_CROSSOVER is strongest in clean directional regimes, while its worst behavior occurs in compression and countertrend conditions.

You discovered that countertrend signals are horrible, but you also discovered that extreme extension itself creates wick-out danger.

Therefore I would divide the trend regime into two pieces.

### Normal momentum

```text

```

**Trade full-size.**

### Extended / exhausted momentum

```text

```

**Do not chase the crossover.**

Wait for a pullback/retest or simply stand aside.

Your reports contain several candidate boundaries around 0.85%, 2.0%, 2.2% and 2.5% depending on the particular analysis.  

I would **not pick one of those numbers as “the true value” yet**. They are candidate thresholds that need an explicit walk-forward sweep.

This is the biggest improvement I would make.

A crossover alone tells you:

> the short-term relationship between two EMAs changed.

It does **not** tell you whether the market has enough immediate velocity to complete your scalp.

Your winning trades tend to resolve extraordinarily quickly, while stagnant trades become toxic. 

Therefore your EMA signal should require something like:

```text

+
macro alignment
+
viable ATR
+
acceptable CHOP
+
acceptable volume
+
no exhaustion wick
```

But crucially:

**these should be viewed as a hierarchy, not equal-weight AND conditions.**

I'd structure them:

### Hard vetoes

* ATR below target-feasibility threshold
* wrong side of EMA200 during strong trend

* extreme volatility/climax

### Soft confirmations

* volume participation

This preserves signal density.

# 7. EMA exit architecture

This should be completely replaced.

The 90-second timeout is dead.

The evidence suggests the useful region is approximately 35-45 seconds, with several reports converging around 40 seconds. 

My first production candidate would therefore be:

```text
T = 0s
    enter

T = 15s
    inspect MFE

T = 18s
    if MFE >= +1 tick and trade has retraced:
        move protection to breakeven

T = 25s
    if MFE < +1 tick and PnL <= 0:
        scratch/kill

T = 35s
    if still not moving decisively:
        aggressive exit

T = 40s
    absolute market kill
```

The reports independently converge on essentially this type of lifecycle, including +1 tick MFE breakeven activation and a 40-second hard kill.

# 8. One change I would make to the proposed EMA architecture

The reports sometimes use:

> “if +1 tick MFE, move stop to breakeven”

I would **not immediately force this on every trade**.

Why?

Because your target itself is +2 ticks.

A trade reaching +1 tick has demonstrated some momentum, but that does not prove the remaining +1 tick is likely. Moving the stop to exact entry could convert many otherwise profitable trades into scratches.

So I would test three variants:

### Variant A

```text
+1 tick MFE after 18s → BE
```

### Variant B

```text
+1 tick MFE after 15-18s
→ stop at -0.5 tick / near-entry
```

### Variant C

```text
+1 tick MFE
→ trailing stop at +0.25 to +0.5 tick
```

Do not assume A is automatically superior.

That's an experiment the existing reports did **not** conclusively establish.

# 19. Your current 75x leverage architecture deserves a separate redesign

This is actually deeper than either strategy.

Your current configuration couples:

```text
75x leverage
+
-25% ROE stop
+
fixed +2 tick target
```

That means the absolute stop changes as price changes.

Your own research demonstrates the consequence. The effective payoff profile changes materially across the eight-month period. 

I would therefore separate three things:

### Signal

“How likely is this trade to move?”

### Position risk

“How much USDT am I willing to lose?”

### Leverage

“How much margin is required to express that risk?”

Those should no longer be mathematically entangled.

Eventually I'd like your system to define risk like:

```text
risk_budget = fixed fraction of account
stop_distance = market-dependent
position_size = risk_budget / stop_distance
leverage = execution constraint
```

rather than:

```text
75x -> -25% ROE -> whatever tick distance happens to result
```

Your current data strongly suggests that the latter is producing undesirable regime-dependent payoff distortion.

This part is my architectural inference from the backtests, rather than something the reports directly validated.
